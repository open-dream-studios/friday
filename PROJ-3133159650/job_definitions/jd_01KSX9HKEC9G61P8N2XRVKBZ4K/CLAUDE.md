# Addition

## What This Job Type Is

New-square-footage construction attached to an existing home: room additions, sunrooms/four-season rooms, additions over storage or walkout levels, and the decks/stairs/flatwork that come with them. The defining property vs. a remodel: an addition creates new structure (foundation → shell → weathertight → trades → finishes) AND ties into an existing building — the tie-in points (existing wall, existing roof, existing footing) are where the schedule risk lives.

## Sequencing Notes

Phase flow follows the Addition composition: logistics (permits + procurement) runs ahead of and parallel to the build; then demolition → site_foundation → framing_shell → exterior_finishes; rough_trades → rough_inspections → insulation_drywall → interior_finishes run once the shell is weathertight; concrete_landscaping and closeout finish the job. Retrofit work on the existing structure (wall openings for new windows/doors, door replacements) rides with demolition/framing, not as a separate mobilization.

- The shell must be weathertight (roofing, windows, doors, housewrap) before insulation or drywall is scheduled.
- Rough inspections gate insulation — never schedule insulation on the rough-trade finish date without inspection slack.
- Walkout/sloped-lot additions add below-grade work (waterproofing, drainage board, foundation drain to daylight, backfill) between foundation and framing — flat-lot schedules routinely miss it.
- Roof tie-ins to the existing house ("over-framing on existing roof") are concealed-condition territory: existing rafter condition is unknown until opened. Document with photos on day one of roof framing.

## Procurement & Lead Times

- Windows and exterior doors: order at least 21 calendar days before demolition starts. Stock vinyl double-hungs are the norm; non-standard sizes (verify anything that isn't 3050/3030-class) push lead time.

- Engineered lumber (LVL/GLB): often value-engineered out of small additions in favor of dimensional headers; when engineering adds one mid-job it arrives as a change order — flag any "beams sized per IRC, LVL omitted" language as a watch item.
- Mini-split/HVAC equipment: allowance-based; confirm existing electrical service capacity BEFORE ordering the subpanel/feeder package.
- Trex/composite decking + railing: order with the framing package.

## Permits & Inspections

- Building permit: always.
- Mechanical permit: whenever HVAC (mini-split) is in scope.
- Electrical rough + final: whenever a subpanel/new circuits are in scope; a subpanel rides the building permit, but a MAIN service upgrade is a separate permit and is excluded unless post-inspection findings force it.
- Plumbing permit: only when plumbing is actually in scope — sunroom/storage additions usually have none; confirm absence with the owner rather than assuming.
- TDEC septic evaluation: triggered by any ADDED bedroom or bathroom on septic properties. A storage level under 5' clear height is not habitable space and does not trigger it.

## Common Change-Order Risks

1. Roof tie-in concealed conditions (existing rafters, rot at the connection).
2. Underpinning/support of the existing footing when the new foundation ties to it — engineering coordination, rarely fully quantified in base scope.
3. Rock excavation (regional constant — see company rules).
4. Existing-wall surprises when cutting new openings (framing inadequacy, concealed utilities).
5. LVL/engineered members appearing after final engineering.
6. Matching existing finishes (siding profile/color, roofing) — age and discontinued products.
